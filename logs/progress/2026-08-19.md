## A1 and A2 were the same bug, and there was a third

The plan describes two independent defects with two independent patches (§2.1, §2.2). Built that way they fight each other at shared page boundaries, because both are one question asked twice: *which section owns this character?*

The implementation makes every section a **cut point** instead. Every outline node becomes a section — not only leaves — plus a synthetic span for the pages before the first bookmark. Each section then runs from its own heading anchor to the **next section's anchor**, not to a page boundary. A1 and A2 both fall out of that, and the result is provably lossless: every character of every page is claimed by exactly one section. That property is what makes §2.3's coverage assertion mean something rather than approximate something.

Page *ranges* (`page_start`/`page_end`) still describe only a section's own pages, so they remain a partition — `map_page_to_leaf` and stored citations both depend on that.

**A third defect, not in the plan, found by the tests.** Every section hands its page-top text to its *predecessor*. The first section has no predecessor. So a first bookmark sitting partway down page 0 silently dropped everything above it — the same defect as A2, at the one seam A2's logic cannot reach. Fixed by forcing the first section's cut offset to 0. It was worth 141 characters on the October errata (96.2% → 100%) and is a landmine on any future document whose first bookmark is not at the top of its page.

### The §6 gate band is wrong, and was left wrong

7,029 exceeds the 4,500–6,000 band. Two reasons, neither a defect:

1. The band was set against the phantom 6,756 baseline. v2 contains three documents the baseline never had — 3,587 chunks on their own.
2. **§3.5's model is structurally wrong.** It divides total narrative tokens by chunk stride, which assumes chunking spans section boundaries. It does not — chunking is per-section, so each of the corpus's **2,428 narrative sections** yields at least one chunk regardless of size. The 2023-2025 WMP has 959 sections producing 1,591 chunks: 1.66 per section, meaning most sections are already under 512 tokens. That floor is most of the gap.

The band needs re-deriving from section counts, not token counts. Flagged rather than silently adjusted to fit the result.

### Deviations worth knowing

1. **§3.2 and §6 contradict each other on chunk size.** §3.2 decides 512/64 and explicitly retracts 1000; §6 step 10 still says "1000/120". Used **512/64** per §3.2, treating §6's line as a leftover from the retracted draft.
2. **New dependency: `onnxruntime`.** Docling's OCR stage runs RapidOCR, which without onnxruntime falls back to a torch engine that downloads from `modelscope.cn` — unreachable from here (DNS timeout). Structured extraction failed on **every** PDF until this was installed; RapidOCR then uses its bundled ONNX weights offline. This is a live fragility: the ingest pipeline had an undeclared dependency on a reachable Chinese CDN.
3. **Parent expansion de-duplicates by section** (not in §3.3). Two top-k hits from one section would each pull the same ~2,500 tokens. Only the first expands; later hits keep their own chunk and their ranking signal.
4. **Container kept its name.** §5 calls the baseline `sdge-pg-baseline`; on disk it is `sdge-postgres` (volume `sdge_pgdata`). Renaming a live container for cosmetics was not worth the risk.
5. **§10.2 was already fixed** in an uncommitted change from the prior session. Verified: all 18 golden-set source paths resolve on disk.
6. Narrative `max_tokens` is 514, not 512 — re-tokenising the stripped piece adds up to two tokens versus the offset window. Harmless against a 32k-context model.
