# 2026-08-19 (extended) — fixing the Excel lane

Continues `2026-08-19.md`. That day ended with the Excel lane diagnosed as
"semantic, not structural". **That conclusion was wrong.** The lane was broken
in five specific, mechanical ways, three of which produced confidently *wrong
numbers* rather than refusals. This session found them, fixed them, and
measured the result on the 16 workbook-dependent beta cases and on a held-out
set written afterwards.

Nothing is committed. `config/config.yaml` and `.env` are unchanged.

---

## At a glance

**The problem.** Excel questions are analytic queries over a relational fact
store (31,833 facts, 58,157 records), and the lane was answering them by
vector-retrieving descriptive cards. Worse, when it did build a query, three
separate defects made that query return the wrong rows *and report itself as
verified*.

**Seven changes, in dependency order.**

| # | change | why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trace every plan, its compiled SQL, its rows, and every rejection reason | A run recorded only `verified_excel: true`, so a wrong figure could not be attributed. Every fix below was found by reading this, not by guessing. |
| 2 | Numeric casts on jsonb filters, ordering without a cast refused | `->>` returns text; `'2.0' > '1000'` is true. Filters were silently returning wrong rows. |
| 3 | Widen the typed DSL: jsonb `group_by`, record aggregates, `HAVING`, `IN`/`NOT IN`, `count_null` | The DSL could not express "break down by initiative", "delayed or cancelled", "beat the target", or the blank cohort. |
| 4 | Delete the regex gate; guard the plans instead | A keyword regex sent 11 of 16 questions to card heuristics that cannot express a `GROUP BY`. |
| 5 | Workbook manifest as standing evidence (~4,300 tokens) | Coverage, queryable schema, and value domains were in the DB and never in a prompt. |
| 6 | Attempt the workbooks once on the whole question when every planner step was routed to PDF | Two cases never reached the Excel lane at all. |
| 7 | Refuse `sum`/`avg` over a record attribute spanning several units | `sum(annual_quant_target)` added Inspections + Structures + Poles + Trees + Miles + Capacitors — 23 units — into one number and reported it. |

**The result.**

| instrument | baseline | final | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beta golden, 16 workbook cases (auto-judge, n=3) | 45.00 | **54.92** | **+9.92** (t=3.60) |
| Held-out, 8 unseen questions (fact coverage, n=3) | 31.0% | **53.2%** | **+22.2 pts** (t=2.91) |
| Run-to-run sd on the beta cases | 4.61 | **1.26** | a third |
| Beta cases below 25 (on the 3-run per-case mean) | 2 | **0** | — |
| Beta cases executing a real query | 11 | **13** | identical in all 3 runs |
| Beta cases never attempting the Excel lane | 2 | **0** | — |

The held-out gain being the larger of the two is the evidence that this is a
real fix rather than a fit to the beta set.

**Three changes measured and reverted** (details below): one-table rendering,
open table selection, and a wider grouped-aggregate limit — each cost about
3.6 points. All three put *more* evidence in front of the model.

**Two instrument defects found, and deliberately not tuned around:**
`excel_006`'s gold denies 2022 data that demonstrably exists in the corpus, and
the held-out `ho_008` gold is probably worse than the answer the system gives.

## What was actually wrong

### 1. Every numeric filter on a jsonb attribute silently returned wrong rows

`compile_plan` emitted `t.attributes ->> %s <op> %s` and coerced the value with
`str()`. `->>` returns **text**, so these were string comparisons. Measured
against the live v2 corpus:

| filter | returned | correct |
|---|---|---|
| `annual_quant_target = 0` | **4** | 10 |
| `quant_actual_progress_q1_4 > 1000` | **102** | 41 |

The second is the dangerous one: `'2.0' > '1000'` is true lexicographically, so
the executor returned rows matching nothing the caller asked for. Because those
rows were non-empty, the channel's generate-and-verify gate reported
`verified_excel = true`. The answering model was being grounded in a query that
lied to it — which is exactly the signature the previous session recorded and
could not explain: `groundedness 2.8` sitting on top of `correctness 1.5`.

### 2. A regex gate starved the planner on 11 of the 16 questions

`question_needs_model_shapes()` was a keyword regex. Every "break down by",
"did we ever", "is it possible that" question fell through to card heuristics
that cannot express a `GROUP BY` or a predicate scan. Same failure as
`_EXCEL_RE` and `MIN_CARD_SCORE` before it: a hand-written classifier starving
the one component able to answer.

### 3. Card-derived plans anchored to whatever ranked first

`excel_004` asks whether any activity reported completed work against a zero
target — a scan over all 128 activities. The channel saw the top card was an
activity, saw the word "target" in the question, and returned **the three-year
history of WMP.1191**, an unrelated activity, marked execution-verified. The
model read it and correctly concluded from that evidence that no such case
existed. The gold lists seven.

### 4. The DSL could not express the questions

- `group_by` compiled as `t.{column}` — typed columns only, never a jsonb key.
  "Biggest capital programs" grouped by `record_id`, returning opaque hashes.
- `VALUE_COLUMN[RECORDS] = None`, so on tables 1 and 13 the only aggregate was
  `count(*)`. No sum or average of any numeric attribute.
- No `HAVING`, no `IN`, no null cohort.

### 5. The schema was in the database and never shown to anyone

`excel_revisions.column_inventory` and the contracts' entity mappings were
populated and never reached a prompt. The planner did not know that table 13's
`status` column *is* the GO 95 Rule 18 priority, so for `excel_002` it
hallucinated "Level 2" into `hftd_tier = 'HFTD Tier 2'` — a different concept —
returning 29,084/15,698/5,005 against a gold of 1,923/3,230/2,292.

## What changed

### Phase 0 — instrumentation

`retrieval/query/excel/trace.py`, `eval/excel_lane_report.py`. A run recorded
`verified_excel: true` and nothing else, so a wrong figure could not be
attributed. Traces now capture the plan, the compiled SQL, the bound
parameters, the returned rows, and every planner rejection reason. **Every fix
below was found by reading that report, not by guessing.**

### Phase 1 — numeric casts

`Filter.cast` with a code allowlist (`numeric`/`date`/`text`). Numeric casts
compile to a regex-guarded `CASE WHEN ... ~ ... END::numeric`, so a blank or
non-numeric attribute yields NULL instead of aborting the statement. An
ordering operator on a jsonb attribute **without** a cast is now refused
outright rather than compiled as text.

### Phase 2 — DSL widening

`GroupKey` (jsonb group keys, generated aliases, key bound as a parameter),
`value_json_key` / `value_column` (aggregates over record attributes),
`Having`, `count_null` / `count_distinct`, and `in` / `not_in` compiling to
`= ANY(%s)` / `<> ALL(%s)`.

Group and order clauses use select-list **ordinals** rather than repeating the
expression, so a jsonb group key binds its parameter exactly once.

`_compile_filter` is now shared by the main statement and by both metadata
scope queries. They previously compiled filters separately and had already
drifted: adding `in` worked in the result and raised `KeyError` while gathering
that same result's unit and provenance metadata.

`sum(*)` / `avg(*)` — which a record-table plan with no value target used to
produce — are refused instead of being sent to the database as invalid SQL.

### Phase 3 — remove the gates, guard the plans instead

- Every Excel-lane question now reaches the model planner.
- A plan may not filter `entity_key` to an entity the question does not name.
- A failed *primary* plan no longer discards valid follow-ups. Each body stands
  or falls alone; one bad first guess was throwing away queries that had
  already validated and executed.
- Card entity-history requires the question to actually name the activity, not
  merely to contain the word "target".
- Over-strict guards became **scoping** rather than rejection: a plan with no
  period gets grouped by `reporting_year`; a fact plan with no metric key gets
  grouped by `semantic_metric_key`. The guard's purpose (never silently
  conflate) is served by labelling, and the question gets answered.
- Mixed units no longer abort a plan that groups by metric — that is a
  legitimate breakdown, and it now carries a warning not to total across rows.
- `count` on a fact table becomes `sum`: one fact row is one metric observation
  for one period, so counting rows counts reporting slots, never events.
  Counting returned 96 for an ignition driver whose 2024 total was 14.
- A decomposed question whose every step was routed to PDF now attempts the
  workbooks once on the whole question. `real_004` and `real_008` were never
  reaching the Excel lane at all.

### Phase 4 — workbook manifest

`retrieval/query/excel/manifest.py`. ~4,300 tokens of coverage and schema,
injected as standing evidence and into the planner prompt: per table, the
covered years and quarters, the *queryable* fields (typed columns marked
`[col]`, distinguished from jsonb attributes), low-cardinality value domains
with their blank counts, the metric-key families, and which source column each
typed column was promoted from.

It deliberately reports what is **queryable**, not `column_inventory`: table
13's `go95_rule18_priority` is promoted into `status` during ingest, so
advertising the source column sent the planner after a field that does not
exist. It now reads `column status [col] is the go95 rule18 priority` and
`status [col] values: 'Level 2', 'Level 3'; 50,446 rows blank`.

This is what the 131072-token context budget is actually for. The budget raise
from the previous session fixed a real PDF truncation bug and was inert on
Excel; it now carries the schema and full result sets.

## Three changes measured and reverted

Recorded so they are not retried blind. Each was plausible and each lost.

| change | arm | mean | vs keeping it out |
|---|---|---|---|
| render a result as one table chunk instead of one chunk per row | p8 | 51.22 | −3.6 |
| let the planner select any of the 14 tables, not only retrieved ones | p9 | 51.20 | −3.6 |
| raise the grouped-aggregate default limit 8 → 60 | p9 | (same arm) | — |

The common thread, and it matches the previous session's finding about
deterministic aggregates: **putting more evidence in front of the model made
the answers worse.** A row that is its own chunk is separately citable and
carries its own provenance; a wider table list gives the planner more ways to
be wrong than to be right.

The table-selection change did fix individual choices — "what caused our
ignitions" moved from the metric glossary (table 3) to the ignitions table
(table 6) — and still lost on the mean. The manifest still describes every
table in the prompt, as context for saying what the corpus cannot answer; the
plan is still built on a table retrieval surfaced.

Two fixes made in the same round were kept because they are unambiguous bugs,
and were re-measured together as `p10`: `GroupKey` normalisation in
`_merge_question_dimensions` (it compared a field name against `GroupKey`
objects, so it added a filter on the very dimension being grouped and collapsed
"capital and O&M" to CAPEX alone), and `count`→`sum` on fact tables.

## A defect in the instrument

**`excel_006`'s gold is wrong, and the judge penalises a correct answer.**

The question asks for 2022 inspection actuals. The gold states no 2022 data
exists anywhere in the corpus and marks the item `unanswerable`. It does exist:
`SDG&E_2026-2028_WMP_R2.pdf` page 230, Table 8-2, "Inspection and Findings Per
Year", reports 2022 Distribution Infrared Inspections — 12,264 inspections, 22
findings (17 thermal, 5 visual), 0.139% thermal find rate, $158,931.25.

In `p5_r1` the system answered with exactly that figure, cited the chunk, and
added "the SDG&E quarterly data report tables only cover years 2023-2026" — the
manifest working as designed. The judge scored it 32.50 for fabrication.

The gold's own text says to decline "narrative references to prior-year
performance in the WMP text", so the author scoped the question to the
workbooks. But Table 8-2 is a table of inspection actuals, which is what was
asked for. **This was left alone deliberately.** Tuning the pipeline to refuse
a question it can correctly answer would buy points on this instrument and make
the product worse. It caps the achievable score by roughly 1 point on the
16-case mean.

## Why the effect is only +8.31 — a decomposition

Asked why the movement is small and what actually makes the model answer wrong,
I measured it rather than reasoned about it. Method: extract every numeric
`expected_value` from each gold's `retrieval_information`, then check, per
figure, whether it appears in an executed query's returned rows and whether it
appears in the final answer. 98 gold figures across the 16 cases, three runs.

### Where the gold figures go

| outcome | share | reading |
|---|---:|---|
| stated correctly in the answer | **24.1%** | |
| returned by a query, dropped from the prose | **8.5%** | prose attrition |
| never returned, but derivable by summing 2-4 returned rows | **20.3%** | arithmetic not done |
| never returned, within 5% (dedupe-policy difference) | 0.9% | |
| never touched by any query | **46.1%** | the real gap |

So **two thirds of the loss is upstream of the prose.** My earlier claim that
"the remaining gap is prose that does not use rows already in context" was
wrong: that is only 8.5 points of the 76 lost. Of the figures a query *did*
return, 26% are dropped before the answer — real, but second-order.

### The mechanism: an arity mismatch

- gold answers demand a mean of **6.1 figures** each, up to 20 (`excel_002`)
- the pipeline executes a mean of **1.5 query plans** per question
- each plan returns **one aggregation level of one table**

The golds are analytical narratives that quote a figure, its per-year
breakdown, its total, and a derived percentage — several slices of several
tables. One plan answers one slice. Nothing in the architecture composes them.

The model then has to produce a complete-sounding answer from a partial
evidence set, and fills the gaps. **56% of the numeric claims in the answers
trace to no executed query row at all.** Some of those legitimately come from
the PDF lane; most are inference presented as fact.

### Two concrete shapes of "wrong"

**Right arithmetic, wrong slice.** `excel_003` wants Territory-scoped 2024
CAPEX (473,986) and OPEX (193,968) plus four record-level programme figures.
The pipeline returned all-scope totals (932,371 / 348,249 / 1,280,620) and an
activity-level ranking (429,654 / 243,941 / 150,709). Every number it produced
is a correct sum — of a different subset than the gold chose. Scored 0.33 on
correctness. This is not a retrieval failure and not an arithmetic failure; it
is a failure to pin down which slice the question meant.

**Right shape refused, wrong shape delivered.** `real_009` wants per-activity
three-year cumulative targets (WMP.549: 35/60/5; WMP.473: 84.43/125/28). The
pipeline ran two whole-table sums and returned 2,053,923.98 — which is
Inspections plus Structures plus Poles plus Trees plus Miles plus Capacitors,
**23 distinct units added together**, executed cleanly and reported as an
answer. See the fix below.

### What is *not* the cause

I tested the obvious suspect and it was wrong. The rubric caps correctness at 1
for "a materially wrong or invented number", and the correctness histogram is
duly bimodal — `{0: 11, 1: 26, 2: 1, 3: 1, 4: 9}` over 48 case-runs, almost no
middle. That looks like a step function compressing real progress. It is not:
re-scoring correctness as `4 x (fraction of gold figures stated)` moves the
suite from 53.31 to **54.22**, a difference of +0.91. The step function is not
hiding a system that quietly gets most figures right — mean figure coverage is
genuinely ~33%.

### Follow-on fix: unit safety on record aggregates

`excel_facts` carries a typed `unit` column and has always refused to aggregate
across units. `excel_records` keeps the unit in a sibling jsonb attribute
(`quant_target_units`), so that guard never saw it, and `sum(annual_quant_target)`
over table 1 silently added 23 incompatible units.

`_record_units_in_scope` now resolves the unit attribute from the table
contract and refuses `sum`/`avg` spanning more than one unit, unless the
grouping already puts each row in a single unit (by entity, record, title, or
metric). `min`/`max` are exempt: they return an existing row's value. Grouping
by `entity_key` — which is what `real_009` actually needed — still executes.

This is the same defect class as the jsonb text-comparison bug: a query that
executes, returns a non-null row, passes generate-and-verify, and means
nothing.

### What this implies for the next increment

The ranked list of remaining loss is now measured, not guessed:

1. **46% of gold figures are never fetched.** Composition, not retrieval:
   the pipeline needs to issue several queries per question and reconcile them.
   `MAX_FOLLOW_UP_PLANS` is 2, and the mean actually executed is 1.5.
2. **20% are fetched as components and never added up.** A deterministic
   roll-up over a returned result (totals, per-year subtotals, percent of
   total) would convert these without the model doing arithmetic.
3. **9% are fetched and dropped in the prose.** The only genuinely
   presentational slice, and every attempt to fix it by changing the evidence
   block has lost.

Note that (1) and (2) are both *composition* problems, and together they are
two thirds of the loss. That is the next thing to build, and it is a different
kind of work from anything done in this session.


## Still open

- **`correctness` and `completeness` remain the binding constraint.** The
  queries are right far more often than the prose built from them. Three of the
  four beta regressions (`excel_001`, `real_009`, `combined_002`) are "the rows
  are in context and the answer does not use them all". Every attempt to fix
  that by *presenting more or differently* lost — see the reverted arms — so
  the next attempt should be on the answering prompt, not the evidence block.
- **`ho_005`** picks table 15 (circuit-segment risk) over table 7 (system
  composition) for "how much overhead line in the highest fire-risk areas", is
  refused by the dual-year-axis clarification, then falls back to an unrelated
  PDF figure (615.1 miles scoped for hardening). Wrong table, then an
  unverified number — the clearest remaining generalization gap.
- **`ho_008`** answers 323 (the corpus's own reported past-due work-order
  metric from table 2) where the held-out gold expects 76 (counted by due date
  in the 2025 Q4 snapshot). Arguably the system's answer is the better one; the
  held-out gold is the questionable half. Left as-is and flagged rather than
  quietly rescored.
- **`excel_005`** ("beat a three-year target overall but reported the final year
  delayed") still declines. It needs a per-activity three-year sum compared
  against a per-activity three-year target *and* a status test on the final
  year — a `HAVING` over one grouping combined with a filter on a different
  grouping. It scores 75.83 by declining honestly, so it is not urgent.
- The two graders still differ by ~17 points. Every beta number here is
  auto-judge.
